The Missing Link

The 8 everyday foundations of feeling your best, and how to find the one holding you back.

By Ashwini KrishnanLife & Wellness Coach · Certified Yoga Teacher

A quick note from me

Thank you for taking the Daily Balance Check.

Most people who reach out to me are not lazy or careless. They are busy, and they have already tried hard to eat better, sleep more or move more. Still, something feels off.

What I keep coming back to is this: it is rarely about trying harder. It is about finding the one area that is quietly holding everything else back.

This short guide walks through all eight areas from your check, so you can see the full picture in about ten minutes. Read it with your quiz result in mind.

Ask yourself

Before you scroll on: which of the eight areas do you already suspect is your missing link? Hold that guess. It may surprise you.

You do not have a willpower problem.

Think of your body as a wheel with eight spokes. When all eight are strong, the wheel rolls smoothly. When one is weak, it still turns, but every rotation costs more effort.

That extra effort often shows up as tiredness, cravings or restless nights, and the natural reaction is to push harder. It is also why strict diets and new workout plans tend to fade. They ask for more effort without fixing the weak spot.

The missing link idea

One weak link makes the other seven work harder. Find it, fix it first, and the rest gets easier.

Most advice is written for everyone. You are not everyone, and your weak spot is not your neighbour's.

1

Hydration

What it does

Water carries nutrients around your body and supports your energy through the day.

Where it slips

Thirst shows up only after your body's water level has already dropped by 1 to 2%. On busy days, most of us simply forget.

Start here

Finish one full bottle before lunch, and keep it where you can see it.

Ask yourself

You probably drink less than you think. How would you know for sure?

2

Protein

What it does

Protein is a main building block of muscles, bones, skin and nails, and it keeps you full between meals. A common guide for adults is about 1 g per kg of body weight a day.

Where it slips

Breakfast and snacks are where it usually goes missing: tea with biscuits, or plain toast.

Start here

Add one protein to every meal. Vegetarian: dal, chana, rajma, paneer, curd, tofu, sprouts. If you eat eggs or non-vegetarian food: add eggs, chicken or fish. Vegan: dal, chana, tofu, soy chunks, peanuts.

Ask yourself

Look at yesterday's breakfast. Where was the protein?

3

Fibre

What it does

Fibre supports regular digestion and helps you feel full for longer. Indian dietary guidelines suggest about 25 g for every 2000 kcal you eat.

Where it slips

Refined foods such as maida, white bread and polished rice carry very little of it.

Start here

Swap one refined item today. Atta roti or millets instead of maida, whole fruit instead of juice, and a salad with one meal.

Ask yourself

Do you know how much fibre you ate yesterday? Most people cannot say.

4

Vitamins & minerals

What it does

They keep your energy, immunity and everyday body functions running, and most of them have to come from food.

Where it slips

A 2021 review of studies found that more than half of people in India are short of at least one micronutrient. It is easy to miss.

Start here

Aim for four servings of vegetables and fruit a day, including one leafy green. One serving is about a fist.

Ask yourself

If more than half of people are short of something, how would you find out what you are missing?

5

Healthy fats

What it does

Your body needs fat, and the kind matters more than most people expect.

Where it slips

Deep-fried snacks, bakery items and packaged foods tend to crowd out better fats.

Start here

Swap one fried snack for a handful of nuts. Add walnuts, flax or chia seeds to a meal. If you eat fish, try it once or twice a week for its omega-3 fats.

Ask yourself

Which fats did you eat yesterday, and how many of them did you actually choose?

6

Plant colour

What it does

Plant colours come from phytonutrients, such as lutein in leafy greens and lycopene in tomatoes. Each colour brings something different.

Where it slips

A beige plate of rice, roti and biscuits leaves most of them out.

Start here

Look for four colours in a week: leafy green, orange or yellow (carrot, papaya, pumpkin), red (tomato, beetroot) and purple (brinjal, jamun, berries).

Ask yourself

Which colour do you almost never eat? Why is that?

7

Rest & sleep

What it does

Rest is part of nutrition too. Diet, exercise, work schedules, screens and stress all shape how well you sleep.

Where it slips

Late screens, late caffeine and a bedtime that moves around.

Start here

Pick a fixed bedtime, put your phone away an hour before it, and keep tea and coffee to the first half of the day.

Ask yourself

When did you last go to bed without your phone? What got in the way?

8

Movement

What it does

Movement and balanced eating work as a pair. Regular activity supports your energy, strength and mood.

Where it slips

All-or-nothing thinking: if I cannot do an hour at the gym, I do nothing.

Start here

A 20-minute brisk walk after one meal. Walking, yoga, cycling, dance and sport all count.

Ask yourself

What is the smallest amount of movement you would still do on your worst day?

One link at a time.

Do not try to fix all eight this week. Pick one and start small.

Questions most people ask next

  • How do I fit this around my job, my family and the food we cook at home?
  • How do I know if food alone can close the gap, or when extra nutrition support makes sense?
  • What does a plan built around my day actually look like?

These are the questions we work through together. Every wheel is different, so every plan is too.
